An SLA is only as good as what happens when it is broken. Off chain that is a ticket and an argument. Backstop makes the SLA self-enforcing, paid on evidence.

open_sla(provider, service, status_url, target, window_end, escrow): a buyer escrows the payment against a plain-words target and one public status page, over a future window. settle(sla_id), open to anybody once the window closes, fetches the page and passes the round the target and window. A GenLayer round returns MET, MISSED or UNCLEAR: MET releases the escrow to the provider, MISSED refunds it to the buyer, UNCLEAR leaves it held. Neither side settles it: the provider cannot mark their own uptime, nor the buyer withhold for an outage that never happened. The window is enforced: refused if not future, settle refused before it closes, injected into the round. An unreadable or not-found page is UNCLEAR, moving nothing. balance(address) is releases and refunds received. Proven on Asimov; tests in tests/backstop_rules.py.
