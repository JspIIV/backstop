An SLA is only as good as what happens when it is broken. Off chain that is a ticket and an argument. Backstop makes it self-enforcing, with real money behind it.

open_sla(provider, service, status_url, target, window_end) is payable: a buyer deposits the escrow as native value, held by the contract; the escrow equals the value sent. A buyer cannot open against themselves, and the window must be future. settle(sla_id), open to anybody once the window closes, fetches the page and passes the round the target and window. A round returns MET, MISSED or UNCLEAR: MET transfers the deposit to the provider, MISSED refunds it to the buyer, each exactly once; UNCLEAR leaves it held. An invalid open refunds the deposit; an unreadable page moves nothing.

The escrow path is real: opening deposits the money, the contract holds it, settlement moves it once. Neither side settles it themselves. Proven on GenLayer Studio where value moves; same source on Asimov. Tests in tests/backstop_rules.py.
